Alpha Centauri System

Alpha Centauri is our nearest stellar system: a hierarchical triple made of the Sun-like pair Alpha Centauri A and B, plus the much farther red dwarf Proxima Centauri. This guide separates confirmed planets from current evidence and explains what the 3D visualization is showing.

System architecture

Alpha Centauri A and B form a close binary with an orbital period of about 80 years. Proxima Centauri is bound much farther away and takes hundreds of thousands of years to orbit the inner pair. The displayed Proxima orbit is intentionally compressed so all three stars can be explored in one interactive scene.

What is known

Alpha Centauri AG-type, Sun-like star; about 4.37 light-years away.
Alpha Centauri BK-type companion; cooler and less luminous than A.
Proxima CentauriM-type red dwarf and the closest individual star to the Sun.

Alpha Cen A candidateRecent JWST evidence points to a possible giant planet, but it is not yet confirmed.

Why Proxima b is interesting

Proxima b orbits within the star's habitable-zone distance, but that does not establish that it is habitable. Proxima is an active flare star, and intense radiation and stellar activity could strongly affect any atmosphere.

Alpha Centauri A

Alpha Centauri A is a G-type main-sequence star and the brighter member of the close pair. Its temperature and overall stellar properties are broadly Sun-like, although it is somewhat more massive and luminous than the Sun. It is useful as a nearby comparison object when astronomers study stars similar to our own.

Alpha Centauri B

Alpha Centauri B is a cooler K-type main-sequence star. It is less massive and less luminous than Alpha Centauri A. A and B orbit one another on an approximately 80-year timescale, making their changing separation observable with long-term astronomical measurements.

Proxima Centauri

Proxima Centauri is a small M-type red dwarf. It is physically much farther from the A–B pair than A and B are from each other, even though all three stars belong to the same gravitationally associated system. Proxima is also an active flare star, so its brightness and radiation environment can change significantly.

Proxima Centauri b

Proxima b is a confirmed exoplanet with a short orbital period of roughly 11.2 days. It receives an amount of stellar energy that places its orbit in the broad region often called the habitable zone. That label describes orbital heating conditions only; it does not prove that the planet has an atmosphere, liquid surface water, or life.

Proxima Centauri d

Proxima d is a confirmed small planet on an even shorter orbit of about 5.1 days. Its small estimated mass makes it especially interesting for studies of low-mass planets around nearby red dwarfs. Its close orbit means it receives intense radiation compared with Earth.

Habitable-zone caution

A planet being in a star's habitable zone is only a first screening criterion. Atmospheric composition, magnetic protection, stellar activity, surface conditions, and planetary history all matter. Proxima's frequent stellar activity makes the environment particularly important when evaluating the habitability of its planets.

Why this system matters

Alpha Centauri is one of the most important nearby stellar laboratories because its stars are close enough for detailed observation. The system helps astronomers investigate stellar evolution, binary dynamics, exoplanet detection, red-dwarf activity, and the conditions around nearby planetary systems.

How the 3D model represents reality

The visualization uses a compressed interactive scale. The true separation of Proxima from Alpha Centauri A and B is enormous compared with the A–B separation, so displaying every distance literally would make the inner binary almost impossible to see. Star and planet sizes are also exaggerated for visibility and are not drawn to the same physical scale.

Alpha Centauri Ab (candidate)

Alpha Centauri Ab is shown in the SpaceVerse visualization as a planet candidate associated with Alpha Centauri A. Its status is kept explicitly as “candidate” because the evidence does not justify presenting it as a confirmed exoplanet. The 3D marker and orbit are illustrative and are not drawn to physical scale.

Confirmed vs. candidate objects

SpaceVerse distinguishes confirmed planets from objects that still require additional observations. The Alpha Centauri A planet candidate is intentionally labeled as a candidate in this guide rather than being presented as a confirmed discovery.

Observation notes

Alpha Centauri is located in the southern sky and is a prominent nearby stellar target. Its apparent brightness and close angular separation make careful observation and suitable optics important when attempting to distinguish the individual stars of the A–B pair.

Important visualization note

Stellar sizes, distances and planet sizes are not all shown to the same physical scale. The scene uses a compressed educational scale so the binary, Proxima and its planets can be viewed together without losing the interactive experience.
