TRAPPIST-1 System

A compact system of seven Earth-sized worlds orbiting an ultra-cool red dwarf about 40.7 light-years away in Aquarius. This guide separates measured properties from scientific interpretation and from questions that remain open.

James Webb Space Telescope findings

b & c

Published Webb results have found little evidence for thick atmospheres on both planets. Current results for b are consistent with a very hot, potentially bare-rock world; c, if it has an atmosphere, appears to have a very thin one.

d & e

Webb observations have ruled out thick hydrogen atmospheres, while the detailed atmospheric interpretations remain under study. More transits are needed to separate planetary signals from stellar activity.

f, g & h

NASA's current summary says Webb observations have been collected for all seven planets, but published community results currently cover b, c, d and e; f, g and h remain under analysis.

Habitable zone, water & composition

The broad habitable zone is the range of orbital distances where, under suitable atmospheric conditions, liquid water could potentially exist on a rocky surface. TRAPPIST-1 e is the strongest classic target in this system, with f and g also discussed in the broader habitable-zone context. Being in this zone does not prove that a planet has an atmosphere, oceans, or life.

Water

Water may be possible on some worlds, but the amount, state, and surface accessibility are not directly established by the orbital position alone.

Rocky interiors

Mass, radius and density measurements indicate these are small terrestrial-type planets rather than gas giants.

Atmospheres

The presence, composition and persistence of atmospheres are central open questions and major JWST targets.

Stellar activity

TRAPPIST-1 is an active red dwarf with flares and star spots. Those variations can imprint signals on transit observations, making it harder to distinguish a planet's atmospheric signature from changes produced by the star itself. NASA notes that the activity is more intense than originally expected, so repeated observations are needed.

Tidal effects & orbital architecture

Likely tidal locking

The planets orbit extremely close to their low-luminosity star, making tidal effects important. Many models expect synchronous rotation, but atmospheric circulation can still redistribute heat.

Compact architecture

All seven planetary orbits would fit well inside Mercury's orbit around the Sun. Their closely packed arrangement produces strong planet-to-planet gravitational interactions.

Orbital resonances

The system's chain of near-resonant orbital periods is one reason TRAPPIST-1 is valuable for studying planetary dynamics and formation.

Confirmed facts vs. scientific uncertainty

Well established

Seven planets are known; their transit detections, approximate masses, radii, orbital periods and orbital distances are measured from observations.

Still being tested

Atmospheric composition, clouds, surface pressure, surface water and long-term atmospheric survival require additional observations and modeling.

Do not overinterpret

“Habitable zone” is an orbital-energy criterion, not evidence that a planet is inhabited or definitely has liquid water.
