Gaia BH1 — the closest known black hole

Gaia BH1 is a dormant stellar-mass black hole in a binary system with a Sun-like companion star. It lies about 1,560 light-years from Earth in the direction of Ophiuchus. Its presence was revealed by the companion star's orbital motion measured with high-precision astrometry and then tested with spectroscopic radial-velocity observations.

What is Gaia BH1?

Gaia BH1 is the compact remnant of a once-massive star. The black hole itself has no visible surface, and the system is considered dormant because it is not currently producing the strong accretion-powered radiation commonly associated with active black-hole binaries. It is detectable because its gravity changes the motion of its visible companion.

Structure / Composition

The system contains the black hole and a Sun-like G-type companion. The black hole is estimated at roughly ten solar masses. The visible star remains outside the event horizon and follows a wide, eccentric orbit. The SpaceVerse 3D model is intentionally not to scale; the real size ratio would make the black hole effectively invisible at a useful web viewing distance.

Formation

Gaia BH1 is expected to have formed when a much more massive progenitor star exhausted its nuclear fuel and collapsed under gravity. In a binary system, that collapse can leave a black-hole remnant orbiting a surviving companion. The detailed pre-collapse mass-transfer history and orbital evolution are inferred from stellar-evolution models rather than directly observed.

How scientists discovered it

Gaia's precise astrometry revealed that the visible star undergoes a periodic wobble in the sky that cannot be explained by its normal motion alone. Follow-up spectroscopy measured the companion's radial-velocity variations. Combining the astrometric and spectroscopic orbital solutions showed that the unseen companion is far too massive to be an ordinary star, leading to the Gaia BH1 black-hole interpretation.

Why it matters

Gaia BH1 is important because it demonstrates that dormant black holes can be discovered from gravitational influence rather than bright accretion emission. ESA places Gaia BH1 about 1,560 light-years away and describes it as the nearest known black hole. Such systems provide a way to study the hidden population of stellar-mass black holes and test models of binary evolution.

Current research

Researchers continue refining the orbital solution and stellar parameters, checking for astrometric or spectroscopic systematics, and comparing Gaia BH1 with other Gaia-selected black-hole binaries. Gaia BH2 and Gaia BH3 have expanded this new family of systems, providing a broader sample for investigating black-hole masses, binary histories and how frequently dormant black holes occur in the Milky Way.

What we know

  • Gaia BH1 is a dormant stellar-mass black-hole system in the direction of Ophiuchus.
  • It is about 1,560 light-years from Earth.
  • The published orbital solution gives a black-hole mass of about 9.8 solar masses.
  • The visible companion is a Sun-like G-type star.
  • The orbital period is about 185.6 days and the eccentricity is about 0.454.
  • Its detection combined Gaia astrometry with radial-velocity follow-up.
  • The system lacks the strong accretion-powered X-ray signature expected from an actively feeding binary.

What remains uncertain

The exact masses and orbital parameters carry measurement uncertainties and can be refined with improved observations. The detailed evolutionary history — including progenitor mass, mass transfer, orbital changes and the black hole's formation mechanism — is reconstructed from models rather than observed directly. The SpaceVerse visualization is therefore an educational simulation, not a literal scale reconstruction.
